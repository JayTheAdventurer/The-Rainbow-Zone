Plural Etiquette Questionnaire


This is some info on how to interact with us, and what we'd like all visitors to know.






1. What do you go by collectively? *(a system name, a shared username, group pronouns... etc)*

We go by Rainbow Parade, but most people call us rainbow for short.




2. How should people refer to you?

Using our system name is fine, but it would make us very happy if people referred to our headmates individually.




3. What terms do you prefer for referring to yourselves as individuals, or as a group? Is there any other terminology for yourselves or aspects of your experience that you use and want people to know, or that you dislike and want people to avoid when talking about you?

We would appreciate it if you used "headmate" for individuals, and "system" for our group. Headmate works because the majority of us use the analogy of "living in an apartment complex" to describe our headspace. Some neighbors we know, love, and are involved with daily. Some of them are distant or even hostile, and some of us we don't know about at all.


In general, we don't use the term "alter" for reffering to us, but we'll accept it most of the time. Don't call us identities or parts, please. We do consider ourselves to be different people, especially since we have distinct personalities, diffferent hobbies, and differing identities and opinions about things.




4. Who in your system are people most likely to interact with?

Bluestar or Jay, most likely. Occasionally, Gabe, Sammy, Nurse, Nathan and Greenstar come out to do stuff. The others don't really come out unless they're asked for, have a task to do, or get front stuck. Check out our introductions page if you want to know more about them. We have a lot of co-fronters in our system that prefer to be aware of what's going on, but not interact. Usually we have several headmates co-conscious at once. The main culprits are Sammy, Greenstar, and Nathan, so if you talk to them they most likely will know what is going on. There are more of us that co-front that we haven't introduced yet, maybe we'll do that sometime.




5. Will people be interacting with any child members? How should they treat them? Is there anything they should know when interacting with them?

No. We don't let the littles interact with others on the internet, as they are very vulnerable. The teens might be interested in talking now that we have this site, though. If you do interact with one of our teen headmates, they're probably co-fronting with someone. Some can communicate for themselves (type, talk), and some can only talk with someone else conveying their words for them.




6. Are there any system members who are nonverbal or otherwise have difficulty communicating? What should others expect when speaking with them?

Some of us can't talk, type, or even both. In general, someone will usually be there to help them communicate to others in the physical world. Expect the communication to be a little slow, as conveying the thoughts of another headmate can be taxing on the brain.




7. What should people do if they don't know who's at front?

Please ask! We are not offended at other asking who is fronting. Sometimes it may appear that one host is fronting, but it is actually another headmate masking(or seveal headmates). A stealth mode, if you will. We do it unintentionally sometimes, even though we've been very open online about being plural. It's a hard habit to break.




8. Is it okay for people to ask if they can talk to someone who isn't at front at the moment?

Um? Yes and No. If someone is out, they either wanted or needed to be out at the time. Most of our fronters actually like to be out and about, and would probably be upset if someone wanted to interrupt that just because they wanted to see someone. We can relay the iformation you'd like to say through the person fronting, but we usually won't switch unless there's a need to. Just don't expect a switch to happen unless the headmate actually wants to come out. In short, only certain folks can ask for other headmates in here.




9. If someone talks to one of you, will other system members be aware of the conversation? Will they be actively watching, or just able to remember it later?

In general, assume that the new headmate who's fronting is unaware of the situation. We do communicate between each other pretty well, so they might have a general idea, but sometimes things fall through the cracks.




10. Adding onto the above - if multiple system members will be aware of a conversation, will they want to chime in? If someone wants to speak to a system member one-on-one, what expectations can they have and how should they communicate this?

Sometimes the frequent fronters will want to say something while another is fronting. Bluestar and Greenstar will chime in over each other if they are co-conscious together. We try to respect each other's time up front, but it happens. Instead of the headmate blurting out something over the fronter, the fronter will tell the person that they are conversing with that someone has something to say through third person. It's a lot calmer that way. If a person wants to have a private conversation, just ask the person fronting about it. Most of the time, other headmates will understand this and give some space.




11. How out are you? What should people do when talking to people who don't know you're plural?

We are out online, but private outside of that. We go by Jay's or A's name outside of private spaces. Sometimes we go by Marcus(Bluestar and Greenstar's name). In general, please do not refer to us as a system in public spaces. Pronouns in public can be they/them. In private, please ask about our pronouns, as it changes based on who's fronting. We are open at conventions and meetups, though.




12. Do you have any internal communication difficulties, memory issues, switch triggers, etc that others should be mindful of?

If you say Marcus, Jay or Sammy, they most likely will be listening to you now. They tend to co-front a lot with each other. Actually, saying any headmate's name may get their attention. Jay is especially known for switching at the mention of his name. We sometimes are triggered out by certain songs, belongings, foods and habits. For instance, Bluestar will at least be co-conscious if his favorite songs are played. Jay has a hat he wears specifically that will trigger him out once worn. If someone suddenly switches with the fronter, they most likely will be confused. It doesn't usually look like that, though. We are very used to switching in dangerous situations, so it'll look like nothing really changed(when in actuality, someone just took over the front).




13. Your stance on being asked questions? *(about personal experience, preferences, plurality in general, etc)*

We like talking about our plurality. In fact, we feel like we usually don't talk enough about our plurality. It always feels nice when there's a person who is genuinely trying to understand our point of view. There are some traumatic memories and private stuff that we'd rather not share, but that's it.




14. Is there anything else others should know?

We have a partner system! They're lovely.


We're pretty sure that we're traumagenic in origin. And if we aren't that, then at least the trauma played a big part in why we are the way we are. We think that we're doing alright despite it all.


Also, We are currently not pursuing a diagnosis. There are several reasons for this. The biggest reason is that we think that we don't need the validation from a doctor to know that we exist. There is another large reason why we may not get the diagnosis. Unfortunately, if you are diagnosed with DID/OSDD/PDid, we've heard that doctors tend to refuse attempts to get gender-affirming care. Which sucks, because we're currently looking into getting that at the time of writing this article.
