Glossary


A little glossary list to explain the most commonly used terms on this site.


For a more comprehensive description of terms, please refer to Pluralpedia.





Plural: A plural person is someone who has many identities in one body. We call this state of existence plurality. These entities can be individually distinct [i.e. very different in gender, opinions, hobbies], or be more similar to each other.


System: The grouping of said of people and entities in one body. Systems can be very large or small. Many systems use different terms, as system is often the word used in clinical settings. One such term is collective.


Headmate: The individual identities or people within a system. Alter is the clinical term for this. Some plurals may avoid using the clinical term, choosing to use terms like entities, members, people, and headmate instead of the word alter.


Headspace: The space within a plural system's mind that members can interact with each other in. It also can be reffereed to as an inner world. These spaces can be very vivid within a system's mind, or the system can be unable to visually see anything at all. Every headspace is different, and a headspace isn't required for a system to be plural.


Fronting: When one or more headmates take charge of the body they're in and do the activities and communication in their day to day life. If no one is fronting, some systems go unresponsive or go on autopilot.


Switching: When the current fronting headmate switches with another one. Some systems experience one headmate fully taking over the body, while others feel subtly as if they become someone else. Every system experiences switching differently.


Trigger: A person, item, environment or event that causes a headmate to switch and become the new fronter. These triggers can be positive or negative.


Internal Communication: When headmates are able to communicate with each other without talking or writing something down.


Co-Conscious: When a headmate is paying attention to the front with the current headmate who is fronting. They may be in the headspace, or they are experiencing the outside world through internal communication.


Origin: The reason why a headmate, system, or headspace was formed.


Traumagenic: A headmate or system where their existence originates from trauma. Most commonly seen in systems like DID and OSDD-1, but can be in any type of system.


Mixed-Origin: When a system has several reasons for why they exist. Headmates can differ in origin, as well as headspaces.
